## 1. What it likely is

**Most likely: a scooter-related embedded BLE peripheral, probably an app-connectable control, telemetry, configuration, or diagnostic interface.**

- **Product-class confidence: 85/100.**
- **Brand or model confidence: below 10/100.** No particular scooter brand or model is defensible from this dump.

Evidence:

- The complete local name is **`Scooter1`**, encoded as `53 63 6F 6F 74 65 72 31`. This is the strongest product-class clue, but names are firmware-defined and can be generic, customized, or spoofed.
- **Connectable: Yes** and Flags **`0x06`** indicate a generally discoverable BLE peripheral that accepts connections, rather than merely a non-connectable beacon.
- The sole advertised service is the vendor-specific 128-bit UUID **`86531001-43E6-47B7-9CB0-5FC21D4AE340`**, consistent with a proprietary application interface.
- Manufacturer data uses Bluetooth company ID **`0x04F7`**, decoded by Fieldwatch as **Shenzhen Goodix Technology Co., Ltd.** Goodix is a component/platform supplier, not evidence of a scooter brand.
- Goodix produces general-purpose BLE SoCs for embedded products; for example, its GR551x family supports peripheral and broadcaster roles for IoT and wearable devices. Nothing in this packet establishes that this particular radio uses a GR551x chip.
- There is **no Appearance, model string, standard device-type service, or recognized tracker format** to refine the identification.

Competing hypotheses:

1. A scooter’s main controller, dashboard, or add-on BLE module — best fit.
2. A development board, diagnostic accessory, or another embedded product deliberately named `Scooter1`.
3. A generic Goodix-based IoT peripheral with a misleading or user-assigned name.

A standalone tracker is **not supported by the observed formats**, even though Goodix silicon can be used in many kinds of products.

## 2. Registry / format decode

The raw advertisement separates into these GAP advertising structures:

| Raw bytes | Decode |
| --- | --- |
| `02 01 06` | **Flags**, value `0x06`: LE General Discoverable Mode and BR/EDR Not Supported. Thus BLE-only, not proof that the host product lacks Wi-Fi or cellular. |
| `11 07 40 E3 4A 1D C2 5F B0 9C B7 47 E6 43 01 10 53 86` | AD type `0x07`: complete list of 128-bit service UUIDs. Reversing the on-air byte order gives **`86531001-43E6-47B7-9CB0-5FC21D4AE340`**. This is a custom UUID, not a Bluetooth SIG 16-bit service assignment. |
| `09 FF F7 04 56 A9 20 83 58 DB` | AD type `0xFF`: Manufacturer Specific Data. `F7 04` is little-endian company ID **`0x04F7`**, assigned in the Bluetooth SIG company-identifier registry to Shenzhen Goodix Technology Co., Ltd., as shown in the dump. |
| `0F 09 53 63 6F 6F 74 65 72 31 00 00 00 00 00 00` | AD type `0x09`: Complete Local Name **`Scooter1`**, followed by zero padding. |

Additional registry results:

- **IEEE OUI/CID:** none can be derived. `DB:58:83:20:A9:56` is not an IEEE factory-assigned public address, so an OUI lookup would be invalid.
- **BLE address pattern:** the leading octet `DB` has its two most significant bits set (`11`), consistent with the BLE **static-random address** format. The local bit reported by Fieldwatch agrees that this is not a factory OUI address.
- **Manufacturer payload:** `56 A9 20 83 58 DB` is exactly the advertised address in reverse byte order: `DB:58:83:20:A9:56`. It therefore looks like a firmware-generated address echo, **not an independent serial number**.
- **GAP Appearance:** absent; there is no AD type `0x19`.
- **16-bit service UUIDs:** none advertised.
- **iBeacon:** absent. There is no Apple company ID `0x004C` followed by the iBeacon `02 15` structure, so no proximity UUID, major, or minor exists to decode.
- **Apple Continuity / Find My:** absent; no Apple `0x004C` manufacturer payload or recognizable Continuity type.
- **Google Fast Pair:** absent; no `0xFE2C` service data or three-byte Fast Pair model ID.
- **Eddystone:** absent; no `0xFEAA` service.
- **Microsoft manufacturer format:** absent; company ID is `0x04F7`, not Microsoft’s `0x0006`.

The custom service UUID has no reliable public product association established by this observation.

## 3. What that product typically does

A scooter BLE peripheral commonly provides a phone application with some combination of:

- connection and pairing;
- scooter status or telemetry;
- configuration and control functions;
- diagnostics;
- firmware update or vendor-specific commands.

The advertisement alone does **not** reveal which of these functions this service implements.

Its radio behavior is consistent with a **connectable, discoverable application peripheral**. Such devices may advertise continuously while awake, only during pairing, or intermittently according to power state. The two observed presence windows are insufficient to determine its general advertising duty cycle.

## 7. Open questions

The most useful additional evidence would be:

1. **A documented advertisement from a known scooter model or vendor app/firmware** containing the same service UUID. A byte-for-byte match would materially raise model confidence.
2. **Complete passive captures of advertisements and scan responses on all three BLE advertising channels**, looking for Appearance, model strings, additional services, or changing payloads.
3. **A GATT service/characteristic listing from an independently available app or authorized connection**, especially Device Information Service `0x180A` values. None is present in this capture.
4. **Repeated observations across power cycles**, showing whether the address, reversed-address payload, name, and UUID remain unchanged.
5. **Controlled correlation with a known product’s awake/asleep state.** Matching advertisement presence to that state would support the product-class hypothesis without proving identity.

More phone GPS samples would mainly document the receiver’s path; they would not, by themselves, identify or locate the advertiser.
